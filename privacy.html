The short version

The developer of Ascent collects nothing about you. Your progress, streaks and settings stay on your device. The free version shows ads served by Google AdMob, and Google collects some information to show and measure them, as described below. Ascent Plus removes ads entirely.

What stays on your device

Ascent saves your level results, daily and archive results, streak, part-finished climbs, hint count, Endless statistics and settings in the app's own storage on your device. A summary of them (your streak, recent daily results, your next level, pack progress, totals, your hint count and whether you have Ascent Plus) is shared with Ascent's widgets through storage that only Ascent can read. Siri and Shortcuts answer from the same summary, on your device. None of this is sent to the developer or to any server, and there is no account.

The developer uses no analytics, crash-reporting or tracking service of its own.

Advertising, and what Google collects

If you don't have Ascent Plus, Ascent shows ads from Google AdMob, using the Google Mobile Ads SDK. To serve, limit, measure and protect those ads, Google's SDK collects on your device and sends to Google:

  • Your IP address, used to estimate your approximate location;
  • Device identifiers: the advertising identifier (IDFA), only if you allow tracking, and otherwise an identifier limited to apps from the same developer;
  • Advertising data and interactions, such as which ads were shown, whether they were tapped, and app launches and video views;
  • Diagnostics: crash logs and performance data such as launch time.

The advertising identifier may be used for tracking across other companies' apps and websites only if you allow it when iOS asks. How Google uses this information is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and in Google's Privacy Policy. Ascent asks Google to show only ads suitable for all ages.

Consent and your choices

  • Where the law requires consent for personalised ads — including the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and several US states — Ascent shows Google's consent form before any ad is requested. You can change your answers at any time in Settings > Ad privacy choices.
  • iOS asks whether Ascent may track you. You can change this at any time in the Settings app under Privacy & Security > Tracking. Ascent works exactly the same either way.
  • If you decline, you may still see ads, but they won't be personalised.
  • Ascent Plus turns ads off completely. Ascent stops requesting ads at once, and from the next launch it doesn't start the Google Mobile Ads SDK at all.

Purchases and subscriptions

Ascent Plus is bought through Apple's App Store. Apple handles payment and billing; the developer receives no name, email, payment or contact details. On your device, the app asks Apple's StoreKit only which Ascent Plus plan this Apple Account has and the state of a subscription (for example, whether it will renew, or whether a payment needs attention), so it can unlock Plus and tell you about it. Apple's own sales reports to the developer are aggregated and don't identify you.

Notifications and widgets

The optional daily reminder is scheduled on your device and names that day's ladder. It sends nothing anywhere. Widgets read the summary described above and nothing else.

Children

Ascent is rated 4+ and suitable for all ages, but it is not directed to children under 13. The developer does not knowingly collect information from children, and does not collect any personal information from anyone. Ads are limited to content rated for all ages.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no information about you, there is nothing to access, correct or delete on the developer's side — deleting the app removes everything Ascent stored. For information Google collected through ads, use the choices above or contact Google through its Privacy Policy. Residents of the EU, UK and elsewhere may also complain to their local data protection authority.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the previous wording stays in the public history of the repository that hosts this page.
